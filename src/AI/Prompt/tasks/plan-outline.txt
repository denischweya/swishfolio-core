Outline the site from the "{{kit}}" kit:

1. Pages: give each a key, a title, a slug and its purpose (what the page is for and what it must say). Follow the rules for keys above: reuse the template's page keys where a page plays the same part.
2. Sections: for each page, the kit section units (or dynamic blocks) it uses, in order, each with a one-sentence brief saying what that section says on this page. Prefer the template's own page compositions and adapt them. Keep the social-proof sections (logos, testimonials, stats) those compositions use even when the owner gave none: their pages fill them with the owner's ownerProof or the marked placeholders (rule 3). Leave out other sections the owner has nothing true to fill. When the owner asks for something by name, give it a section that fits: brands or companies they have worked with, or clients, go in a logos unit (or a list or text unit when the kit has no logos unit); reviews or testimonials in a testimonials unit; numbers in a stats unit. Never put them in a section that lists projects or in the portfolio block: those show the site's projects, and only when the business shows past work (item 5).
3. The front page and the navigation order.
4. Shared facts every page will write from, so the pages agree: the business name, the tone of voice, who the site is for, three to six key messages taken from the owner's own facts, and their contact details, address and hours exactly as they gave them (an empty string when they gave none). Never invent facts.
5. Projects: decide whether this business shows examples of its past work. Yes for portfolios, studios, agencies, designers, developers, photographers, architects, builders and trades (their jobs), consultancies and firms (case studies or matters), coaches (client results), caterers and event services (past events). No for businesses whose site is about what they offer now rather than work they did for clients: restaurants, cafes, bakeries and shops, gyms and classes, salons, clinics and the like. When the answer is no, set "show" to false, leave "items" empty and use no section that lists the site's projects (and no portfolio block, and no page for them). When it is yes, keep a section that lists projects where the kit's pages have one, and write three to six sample projects in "items" that fit this business: plausible, modest examples of the kind of work they do, in their voice, with no invented client names, figures or awards, and never named after a client from ownerProof (a list of clients is not a list of projects). If the owner listed real projects, use those instead.

The summary is one or two sentences for the owner saying what the site is and how it is laid out.

Never use an em dash, or an en dash or hyphen with spaces around it, as punctuation: write a comma, a colon or a full stop instead.
