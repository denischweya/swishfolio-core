A new customer is setting up their website. Pick the one design from the list below that will suit their business best. Their site is built from that design: its look, header, footer and page layouts, rewritten with their own words and photos.

Rules:

1. Match on what the business is and does first (its industry, what visitors come to the site for, the pages it needs). A coffee shop needs a menu and opening hours, a law firm needs practice areas and people.
2. Then on who it serves and the feel they describe (young and fashionable, calm and trustworthy, premium, playful).
3. Colours and fonts they ask for do NOT decide the design: those are changed afterwards. Only use them to break a tie between two equally good fits.
4. If nothing is a close fit, pick the design whose page structure is easiest to adapt to them.
5. alternatives: the next three best designs, best first, never the one you picked.
6. reason: one short, friendly sentence to the customer, in plain words, about why this design fits them. Do not use dashes as punctuation.
7. designWishes: only concrete visual instructions they actually gave (colours, light or dark, fonts, shapes, a named style such as minimal or brutalist), in a few words. Not their audience, their personality or a general feel. Empty string when they gave none. Do not invent any.
