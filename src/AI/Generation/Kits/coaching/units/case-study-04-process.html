The approach

Four phases over seven months.

The aim was never to make the team agree more. It was to make disagreement safe, quick and useful, so decisions could happen in the room.

01Month 1

Listen

One-to-one interviews with all seven leaders and twenty staff across the practice. The pattern was clear by the fifth conversation: nobody felt safe disagreeing with the founder’s old guard.

02Month 2

Two days in the hills

An offsite in the Pentland Hills: walking in pairs in the mornings, working sessions in the afternoons. The team named the three decisions they had been avoiding, and made one of them before dinner.

03Months 3 to 6

Team coaching

Monthly half-days on real decisions, with me observing and pausing the room when the old habits crept back in. Fortnightly one-to-one coaching for the chief executive alongside.

04Month 7

Embed

The team wrote a one-page decision charter and set up a monthly fifteen-minute feedback round. Two leaders trained as internal coaches to keep it going.
