I got into software through a broken spreadsheet. It ran a friend’s small business, and fixing it properly turned out to be the most satisfying thing I’d ever done.

My first real job was at a payments startup, where I learned the habits that still shape my work: test the money paths first, make every operation safe to retry and never trust a system you can’t observe.

Leading a platform team taught me the other half: that the best architecture is the one your team can understand, run and change without you. Going independent in 2019 let me bring both to many more products.

Today I work with two teams at a time. I’m in your Slack, in your repo and on your Friday demo, and when the project ends your engineers own every line.
